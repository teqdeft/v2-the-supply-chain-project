The world does not have a scarcity problem. It has a coordination problem.

The Supply Chain Project is building the operating system for the Second Supply Chain™: connecting surplus goods and unused capacity with verified human need. Universities help develop the intelligence, frameworks and evidence that make this possible. We call that the University Impact Engine.

How the Second Supply Chain works

Five moves, and where the university model fits

One sequence, applied to what is at risk and to what is needed, until the two meet.

01See it

Identify unused goods, space, time or capacity, and verified community need.

02Assess it

Score how likely value is to be lost, how urgent the need is, and whether it can actually move.

03Match it

Compare every option on the numbers and connect available resources to verified need.

04Move it

Coordinate the partners who own the trucks, docks and warehouses. A match is complete only when movement is scheduled.

05Measure it

Track value, impact and outcomes, and claim only what the evidence supports.

Reveal & predict

Uncover waste and predict where surplus will appear.

Value & match

Prioritize and match solutions to needs.

Enable execution

Deploy and scale pathway pilots.

Verify impact

Measure outcomes and drive continuous improvement.

Open research questions

Questions we would like help answering

  • Market design. A two-sided matching market with no price signal, clearing inside three time windows: the life of the goods, the life of the need, and the receiver's ability to take delivery. Where does it fail to clear?
  • Scoring. Can surplus risk and need urgency be calibrated as real probabilities across product classes and regions? What data would it take?
  • Decision rights. Our working view is that the leak is an ownership problem, not an information problem. Does naming an owner for every at-risk asset change disposal rates?
  • Visibility and response. All supply chain disruptions and hidden inefficiencies stem from the same place: the lack of visibility to changes in demand and supply, coupled with the inability to respond in time. How is that measured on the second supply chain?
  • Impact measurement. Wellbeing gained per $1,000 of rescued surplus, claimed only at the level of evidence reached. Is it strong enough to replace activity counts?

What is true today

The Supply Chain Project is defining the Second Supply Chain and building its operating system. Three frameworks have been through a structured critique cycle: 85 critiques answered, each printed with its resolution. The model is documented and buildable now.

What still has to be proven

Claims about results wait for pilots with published thresholds. When the pilots run, we publish what they show, including if the answer is no. Independent academic review is the next step, and we welcome it.
